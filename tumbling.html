Tumbling & Trampoline

Tumbling and Trampoline is The Yard's floor, vault and tumble-track programme for tweens and teens, for ages 10 to 16 at Jurong, our High-Performance Arena. Three levels move from rolls, cartwheels and round-offs to back handsprings, aerials and twisting somersaults, ten skills at a time, and the programme keeps its own Bronze, Silver and Gold medal scale.

Move · Learning through motion, building your strength

The confidence sport
for tweens and teens.

Trampoline is its own track at The Yard, so a child can train it instead of artistic gymnastics or alongside it. It is also one of the best places for a 10 to 16 year old to start from scratch, because the first skills arrive quickly, every one of them looks spectacular, and the conditioning underneath them is built in from week one.

Skills that look spectacular

Somersaults, aerials and walkovers, the skills teens actually want to show their friends, built step by step on floor, vault and the tumble track.

Strength to back it up

Many athletes tell us they feel fitter, stronger and more grounded in their body within a single term. Real conditioning sits behind every trick, because foundations come before sophistication in every Yard class.

A clear ten-skill ladder

Every level has a ten-skill matrix scored on the programme's own Bronze, Silver and Gold scale, so progress is visible, and your child's coach calls each move up when the skills are reliably there.

Intermediate

10 to 16 years1 hourEntry by progression or coach placement

Where tumbling gets flight. The ten-skill matrix moves into handspring, one-handed cartwheels, back handspring, piked front somersault, flyspring and a stronger, faster round-off.

What a class looks like

Drills and progressions on floor, vault and tumble track build the speed, shape and air awareness each skill needs before it is attempted unaided, and a coach spots every new skill until it is safe to fly solo. Plateaus happen at this level and they are part of the progression: the structure holds, the conditioning continues, and the breakthrough arrives.

What parents notice

Genuine athleticism: connected tumbling passes, competition-level intensity, and the discipline that comes from training skills most people only ever see on a screen. We teach the tumbling, and what your teen develops is character.

How progress works

Tumbling keeps its own
medal scale.

Tumbling is assessed within The Yard's Skills Matrix assessments three times a year, and it is scored on its own points scale: ten skills per level, each one contributing to a Bronze, Silver or Gold result.

10Skills per level
10 to 14Bronze: assisted or developing technique
15 to 25Silver: greater independence, cleaner shapes
26 to 30Gold: unaided skills with correct technique

A Gold across the matrix is the signal a coach looks for before moving an athlete up a level. Assessments happen in Terms 1, 3 and 5, medals are awarded on the results, and every assessment celebrates effort and progress.

One track or two

Tumbling fits beside artistic gymnastics, or replaces it

Older recreational gymnasts often add Tumbling as a second track, because the tumble track lets them build the big floor skills faster. Teens who never did gymnastics start here, at any ability, and find the round-off, back handspring and somersault arrive in an order that makes sense.

Good to know

Tumbling questions

Which centre runs Tumbling and Trampoline?

Tumbling and Trampoline runs at The Yard's Jurong centre, the same arena that hosts the competitive squads. Bukit Timah, Dempsey and Dover run gymnastics and ninja programmes instead.

What ages is Tumbling and Trampoline for?

Ages 10 to 16. Beginner and Intermediate classes run for one hour and Advanced runs for ninety minutes. The programme is built as a confidence sport for tweens and teens, and it welcomes complete beginners and older starters.

Does my teen need gymnastics experience to start?

No. Beginner starts from rebound jumps, handstand pops, cartwheels and bridge work, which makes it one of the best entry points at The Yard for a 10 to 16 year old starting from scratch.

How is progress measured in Tumbling?

Each level has a ten-skill matrix, and Tumbling uses its own scoring scale within the Skills Matrix assessments: Bronze for 10 to 14 points, Silver for 15 to 25 and Gold for 26 to 30. Coaches move athletes up when the skills are mastered.

Can my child do Tumbling alongside artistic gymnastics?

Yes. Trampoline is its own track, so a child can train Tumbling and Trampoline instead of artistic gymnastics or alongside it. It works well as a second track for older recreational gymnasts.
