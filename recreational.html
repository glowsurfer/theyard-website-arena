Recreational gymnastics · All four centres · All ages and abilities welcome

One class a week changes a child

Recreational gymnastics is where almost every Yard member begins. Gym Tots (18 months to 3), KinderTots (3 to 4) and FUNdamentals (5 to 10) run at all four Singapore centres, and the pathway continues through Warriors, Senior Warriors and the boys' classes at selected centres. Your child's coach calls every move up, and every skill is medalled Bronze, Silver and Gold at assessments three times a year.

Fueled with fun, designed for progress

The foundation under
every sport they will try.

Every class is built the same way at every centre: warm-up, stretching, apparatus rotations and cool-down, with about one coach to eight children (one to six in KinderTots) on FIG-certified apparatus. Fun is designed into the structure, because a class children enjoy is a class they come back to and try harder in, and parents tell us the change shows within a term or two.

Strength and coordination

Vault, bars, beam and floor build whole-body strength, balance and body awareness in the same hour. Every rep is a small step and the small steps compound, so a term of them shows up in swimming, football, dance, climbing and whatever else your child chooses later.

Confidence that shows at home

Members' parents tell us the same things: braver on the playground, calmer in new settings, more focused at school and prouder of what they can do. The first unaided cartwheel is a moment families remember, and so is the way the coach handled the fall the week before.

Progress you can see

Each level has a 20-skill Skills Matrix, and skills are medalled Bronze, Silver and Gold at assessments three times a year. Your child's coach calls every level move, so a promotion is earned. Progress is the promise, and the pace belongs to the child.

Gym Tots

18 months to 3 years1 hourParent and child together

The class where most Yard members take their first step, with you on the floor beside them. Toddlers and an adult explore obstacle courses and movement stations together, which builds early confidence, body awareness and the listening skills that set children up for pre-school, sport and friendships. It is also a special hour of the week for the grown-up who joins in.

What a class looks like

Play-based stations and obstacle courses, with strength, coordination and flexibility developing through play. Balance and listening come from following the coach's cues, and a coach who knows your child by name is on the floor for all of it.

KinderTots

3 to 4 years1 hourCoach-led, six children to a coach

Their first class without you on the floor, which is a milestone in its own right. A coach leads 3 and 4 year olds through bars, beam, vault and floor with games and stations, six children to a coach, and children leave more confident, more capable and proud of what they tried on their own.

What parents notice

More willingness to attempt new things and more calm in unfamiliar settings. KinderTots focuses on motor movement, so strength, balance, listening and coordination grow through play, and formal skills testing waits until FUNdamentals.

Next step: FUNdamentals at age 5. Boys can also take Boys Tots at Dempsey and Dover.

FUNdamentals

5 to 10 years1 hourAbout eight children to a coach, grouped by age and ability

The core class of the club and the foundation of the whole Yard pathway. FUNdamentals gives children the base that supports every sport they will ever play, and the confidence that comes from discovering they can do hard things one small step at a time.

What a class looks like

Children rotate through vault, bars, beam and floor in a structured, fun session. Coaches split the 5 to 10 band into smaller groups by age and ability, so a five year old and a nine year old each work at the edge of what they can do, with the strain kept out.

Skills and assessment

A 20-skill Skills Matrix covers forward and backward rolls, cartwheel, handstand, bridge, beam balances and walks, vault run-and-jump, chin-ups and swings on bars. Children master 18 of the 20 skills to advance, foundations come before sophistication, and every skill is medalled at assessments three times a year (how the Skills Matrix works).

Warriors & Senior Warriors

Warriors 5 to 10 · Senior Warriors 10 to 1690 minutesEntry by assessment or progression

The first advanced recreational step above FUNdamentals, where children stop simply doing gymnastics and start owning it. Ninety-minute sessions raise the difficulty across vault, bars, beam and floor while raising the bar on technique: handstands, round-offs, bridge kick-overs, beam leaps and forward rolls on the beam, handstand flat-back vaults, upward circles and glide swings on bars.

Senior Warriors is the 10 to 16 stream at Dover, the All-Rounder of our four centres, and it gives older starters and teens an entry point with the strength and technique demands to match their age.

What parents notice

Sharper technique, more independence and the focus to set their own goals, along with a visible lift in self-discipline and resilience.

Boys' classes

Boys Tots 3 to 4 · Boys FUNdamentals 5 to 101 hourBoys' apparatus from the start

Boys' versions of KinderTots and FUNdamentals, trained on the boys' apparatus from day one. The boys' Skills Matrix adds parallel bars, rings and pommel (mushroom) work alongside floor, vault and bars: dips and swings, skin-the-cat, inverted hangs and circles.

Boys' pathway: Gym Tots, then Boys Tots, then Boys FUNdamentals, then Gladiator, then the MAG competitive pathway by selection.

Heroes, Legends & Gladiator

90 minutesEntry by progressionYour coach tells you when

The upper recreational levels, reached by progression from Warriors and Boys FUNdamentals. Heroes brings higher-level skills and more complex routines, including one-hand cartwheels, handsprings, front tuck dismounts and back hip circles. Legends is the peak of recreational gymnastics: back handsprings, aerials, walkovers and somersaults at competition-level intensity. Gladiator is the boys' advanced level across parallel bars, rings and pommel alongside floor, vault and bars.

These levels run as pathway levels, and there is no fixed timeline for reaching them. Your coach will tell you when your child is ready, and the centre team can confirm current session times.

Pathway levels open by coach progression. Ask the team where your child fits today.
Medals
Developing character, skills for life

The Skills Matrix
and the medals.

Foundations before sophistication, every time. A coach decides every level move, so promotion is earned and nobody is rushed, plateaus are treated as a normal part of learning, and the medal system celebrates effort and progress.

20 skills per level

Eight on floor, four on bars, four on vault and trampoline, four on beam. Boys add parallel bars, rings and pommel to the mix.

Bronze, Silver and Gold

Bronze is a skill done with coach support, Silver is done independently with good technique, and Gold is done with excellent form and confidence.

Three assessments a year

Terms 1, 3 and 5, with results within two weeks, medals awarded and a clear picture of what comes next.

18 of 20 to level up

Master 18 of the 20 skills and the coach moves your child up. Levels are earned by demonstrated skill, and some children spend two terms at a level because that depth is what makes the next level stick.

Every level from FUNdamentals up has its own 20-skill Skills Matrix, organised by apparatus. Coaches assess each skill as Bronze, Silver or Gold three times a year, and a child moves up when 18 of the 20 are secure. Gym Tots and KinderTots are play-based and have no formal testing.

Before the first class

Everything you need
to know for day one.

Most children settle faster when the parent watches from the viewing area. The centre team will show you where everything is when you arrive and introduce your child's coach.

What to wear

Close-fitting athletic wear such as a leotard, leggings, fitted shorts or a t-shirt. Hair tied back, bare feet or gymnastics shoes, and no jewellery, zips or skirts.

When to arrive

Ten minutes early, so your child joins the warm-up from the start. Every class is nut-free, and water bottles stay off the gym floor at the designated station.

Where you sit

Every centre has a viewing area where you can see the whole class. We ask for no filming or photography, and your coach can usually take a photo of your child at the end.

Good to know

Recreational questions

Does my child need experience to start?

No experience is needed at any entry level, and all ages and abilities are welcome. Children can join at any time of year, with fees pro-rated for the remaining classes in the term, and their coach places them in the right level. Children move up when they show the next level's skills reliably, and some spend two terms at a level because that depth is what makes the next one stick.

How do Skills Matrix assessments and medals work?

From age 5, each recreational level follows a 20-skill Skills Matrix: 8 floor, 4 bars, 4 vault and trampoline, 4 beam. Each skill is medalled Bronze (done with coach support), Silver (done independently with good technique) or Gold (done with excellent form and confidence). Assessments run three times a year in Terms 1, 3 and 5, results arrive within two weeks and medals are awarded to celebrate effort and progress. A child masters 18 of the 20 skills to advance a level.

Which centres run which recreational classes?

Gym Tots, KinderTots and FUNdamentals run at all four centres: Jurong, Bukit Timah, Dempsey and Dover. Warriors runs at Bukit Timah and Dover. Senior Warriors (10 to 16) runs at Dover. Boys Tots and Boys FUNdamentals run at Dempsey and Dover.

Does my child need to be potty trained?

For KinderTots and every level above it, yes, because children attend without a parent on the floor. Gym Tots has no potty-training requirement because a parent or caregiver joins the child for the whole class.

Can parents watch the class?

Yes. Every centre has a dedicated viewing area so you can see the class without being on the floor. We ask that no photos or videos are taken, for the safeguarding of all children; your coach can usually take a photo of your child for you at the end of class.

How does a child move from recreational to competitive?

Selection is coach-led. Children who progress well in recreational classes can be put forward for a free competitive assessment, and suitable gymnasts are invited into a WAG or MAG squad at the appropriate SGLP and USAG level. Parents can also ask their coach for a conversation at any time. How the competitive pathway works.
