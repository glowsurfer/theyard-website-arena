Recreational Gymnastics

Recreational gymnastics is The Yard's core program: Gym Tots (18 months–3), KinderTots (3–4) and FUNdamentals (5–10) run at all four Singapore centres, progressing to Warriors, Senior Warriors and beyond. Skills are medalled Bronze, Silver and Gold at assessments three times a year.

Why it works

The foundation for
every sport

Every class runs warm-up → stretching → apparatus rotations → cool-down, with roughly 1 coach to 8 children (1:6 for KinderTots) on FIG-certified apparatus.

Strength & coordination

Vault, bars, beam and floor build whole-body strength, balance and body awareness that transfer to every sport they'll ever try.

Confidence that shows

Parents report children braver on the playground, more focused at school and prouder of what they can do — often within a term or two.

Progress you can see

A 20-skill Skills Matrix per level, medalled Bronze, Silver and Gold three times a year. A coach calls every level move — levels are earned, not time-served.

Gym Tots

18 months – 3 years1 hourParent & child

The Yard's parent-and-child entry class. Toddlers and an adult explore obstacle courses and movement stations together on the gym floor — a special hour together that builds early confidence, body awareness and the listening skills that set children up for pre-school, sports and friendships.

What a class looks like

Play-based stations and obstacle courses. Strength, coordination and flexibility develop through play; balance and listening skills come from following the coach's cues.

KinderTots

3 – 4 years1 hourCoach-led, 1:6 ratio

Their first independent class — a coach-led session without mum or dad on the floor. Children explore bars, beam, vault and floor through games and stations, and leave more confident, more capable and proud of what they tried on their own.

What parents notice

More willingness to attempt new things and more calm in unfamiliar settings. KinderTots focuses on motor movement — strength, balance, listening and coordination develop naturally through play, without formal skills testing.

Progression: KinderTots → FUNdamentals at age 5. Boys can also take Boys Tots.

FUNdamentals

5 – 10 years1 hour~1:8 ratio, grouped by age & ability

The core recreational class and the athletic foundation of the whole Yard pathway — the base that supports every sport and physical activity your child will ever do, and the confidence that comes from discovering they can do hard things.

What a class looks like

Children rotate through vault, bars, beam and floor in a structured, fun session; coaches split the 5–10 band into smaller groups by age and ability.

Skills & assessment

A 20-skill Skills Matrix — forward and backward rolls, cartwheel, handstand, bridge, beam balances, vault run-and-jump, chin-ups and swings on bars — with 18 of 20 mastered to advance. Medalled at assessments three times a year (how the Skills Matrix works →).

Warriors & Senior Warriors

Warriors 5–10 · Senior Warriors 10–161.5 hoursEntry by assessment or progression

The first advanced-recreational step above FUNdamentals — where children stop simply doing gymnastics and start owning it. Ninety-minute sessions raise the difficulty across vault, bars, beam and floor while raising the bar on technique: handstands, round offs, bridge kick-overs, beam leaps, handstand flat-back vaults, upward circles and glide swings.

Senior Warriors is the 10–16 stream at Dover — an age-appropriate entry and continuation point for older starters and teens.

What parents notice

Sharper technique, more independence, and the focus to set their own goals — a visible lift in self-discipline and resilience.

Boys classes

Boys Tots 3–4 · Boys Fundamentals 5–101 hour

Boys-specific recreational classes on boys' apparatus from the start — the boys' Skills Matrix adds parallel bars, rings and pommel (mushroom) work alongside floor, vault and bars: dips and swings, skin-the-cat, inverted hangs, circles.

Boys pathway: Gym Tots → Boys Tots → Boys Fundamentals → Gladiator → MAG competitive pathway by selection.

Heroes, Legends & Gladiator

1.5 hoursEntry by progression — ask the team

The upper recreational levels, reached by progression rather than open booking:

  • Heroes — higher-level skills and more complex routines: one-hand cartwheels, handsprings, front tuck dismounts, back hip circles.
  • Legends — the peak of recreational gymnastics: back handsprings, aerials and walkovers at competition-level intensity.
  • Gladiator (boys) — advanced boys' training across parallel bars, rings and pommel alongside floor, vault and bars.

These run as pathway levels — your coach will tell you when your child is ready, and the centre team can confirm current session times.

Good to know

Recreational FAQs

Does my child need experience to start?

No experience is needed at any entry level. Children can join at any time of year (fees are prorated) and a coach places every child in the right level — levels are moved by demonstrated skills, not time served.

How do Skills Matrix assessments and medals work?

From age 5, each recreational level follows a 20-skill Skills Matrix (8 floor, 4 bars, 4 vault/trampoline, 4 beam). Each skill is medalled Bronze (assisted), Silver (greater independence) or Gold (unaided with correct technique). Assessments run three times a year in Terms 1, 3 and 5, with results within two weeks and medals awarded. 18 of 20 skills must be mastered to advance.

Which centres run which recreational classes?

Gym Tots, KinderTots and FUNdamentals run at all four centres — Jurong, Bukit Timah, Dempsey and Dover. Warriors runs at Bukit Timah and Dover. Senior Warriors (10–16) runs at Dover. Boys Tots and Boys Fundamentals run at Dempsey and Dover.

Does my child need to be potty trained?

For KinderTots and beyond, yes — children attend independently and must be potty trained. Gym Tots has no potty-training requirement because a parent or caregiver joins the child on the floor.

Can parents watch the class?

Yes — every centre has a dedicated viewing area so you can see the class without being on the floor. We ask that no photos or videos are taken, for the safeguarding of all children; your coach can usually take a photo of your child for you at the end of class.

How does a child move from recreational to competitive?

Selection is coach-led. Children who progress well in recreational classes can be put forward for a free competitive assessment, and suitable gymnasts are invited into a WAG or MAG squad at the appropriate SGLP/USAG level.
