The evidence page

Why Gymnastics?

Gymnastics is called "the mother of all sports" because it develops every fundamental movement pattern — building all-round strength, balance, coordination and spatial awareness, plus the resilience and focus that carry into school, other sports and the rest of a child's life.

  • Mental Real resilience & confidence
  • Transfer A base for every other sport
  • Academic Executive function & focus
Five kinds of benefit

What gymnastics
actually builds

Physical Mental & emotional Cross-sport transfer Academic & cognitive Life skills
01 — Physical

A whole body,
built at once

Few sports train upper body, core and lower body simultaneously. Gymnastics does it every session — from the very first class.

All-round functional strength

Upper body, core and lower body develop together — alongside flexibility, balance, coordination and spatial awareness, from a young age.

Stronger bones for life

As a high-impact, weight-bearing activity, gymnastics builds 10–40% higher bone density at key sites like the spine and hip — and the benefit persists into adulthood (Ward et al., Osteoporosis International).

Safe falling — a life skill

Rolling and absorbing impact is one of the most transferable skills a child can learn — it reduces injury risk in every sport they'll ever play.

Start early: the WHO recommends gymnastics-type activities for developing fundamental movement skills in children aged 3–5. At The Yard, Gym Tots starts at 18 months.

02 — Mental & emotional

Real courage,
practised weekly

Gymnastics is one of very few sports that asks children to overcome genuine fear — going upside down, leaving the ground — inside a safe, coached environment.

01

Resilience that's real

Inverting and leaving the ground are genuinely scary at first. Conquering that fear — safely, week after week — builds resilience nothing else quite matches.

02

Visible confidence

Roll → handstand → cartwheel → backflip. Skill progression is concrete and visible, so confidence and self-esteem are earned, not told.

03

Growth mindset

Gymnastics teaches that ability is built through effort, not innate talent — the mindset that pays off everywhere.

04

Focus & composure

Sustained focus, discipline, multi-step instructions — plus healthy exposure to structured competition, scoring and handling setbacks.

03 — Cross-sport transfer

The mother of
all sports.

The International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) calls gymnastics "the mother of all sports" because it develops every fundamental movement pattern — locomotion, stability and manipulation.

Children who do gymnastics as a base before specialising in another sport have better long-term athletic outcomes and fewer injuries (Lloyd et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine).

That's why the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends early diversification through activities like gymnastics, rather than early specialisation in one sport before age 12. Gymnastics isn't a detour from your child's future sport — it's the foundation under it.

04 — Academic & cognitive

Stronger in the gym,
sharper in class

The link between movement and learning is well studied — and gymnastics sits at the strong end of it.

Executive function

Cognitively demanding physical activity like gymnastics boosts planning, focus and working memory more than simple exercise like running (Pesce et al., Health Psychology).

Working memory

Learning routines and skill sequences exercises working memory — the same muscle behind following instructions in the classroom.

Classroom carry-over

Physically active children show improved concentration, classroom behaviour and academic performance — a CDC review found a positive link in over half of studies.

05 — Life skills

Lessons that outlast
the leotard

Whether or not a child stays in gymnastics, what they learn on the floor goes with them. That's the design — progress is the point, not pace.

01

Perseverance

Gymnastics has a high attempt-to-mastery ratio — children learn, viscerally, that repeated effort leads to results.

02

Independence

Ultimately a gymnast performs alone. Accountability for your own effort starts early and sticks.

03

Social skills

Group training means taking turns, encouraging peers and respecting coaches — every single session.

04

Time management

Balancing training with school commitments builds habits that serve teens well beyond sport.

Parents ask

Why-gymnastics FAQs

Why is gymnastics called the mother of all sports?

The International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) calls gymnastics the mother of all sports because it develops every fundamental movement pattern — locomotion, stability and manipulation. Children who build this physical-literacy base before specialising in another sport show better long-term athletic outcomes and fewer injuries (Lloyd et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine).

What age should a child start gymnastics?

Early. The WHO recommends gymnastics-type activities for developing fundamental movement skills in children aged 3–5, and the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends early diversification through activities like gymnastics rather than early specialisation in a single sport before age 12. At The Yard, Gym Tots starts at 18 months and no experience is needed at any entry level.

Will gymnastics help my child in other sports?

Yes — the skills transfer directly: aerial awareness to diving, balance and explosive power to martial arts, core strength and body tension to swimming, agility and safe falling to football and rugby, flexibility and body line to dance, grip strength and proprioception to climbing, and explosive power and jumping to athletics.

Does gymnastics help with school and concentration?

Cognitively demanding physical activity like gymnastics boosts executive function — planning, focus and working memory — more than simple exercise like running (Pesce et al., Health Psychology). Learning routines exercises working memory, and a CDC review found a positive link between physical activity and academic performance in over half of studies.

Is gymnastics safe for young children?

In a properly coached environment, gymnastics is one of the best injury-prevention investments a child can make: it teaches safe falling — rolling and absorbing impact — which reduces injury risk in every sport. At The Yard, every class is run by a qualified coach on FIG-certified apparatus at roughly 1 coach to 8 children (1:6 for KinderTots).

My child isn't sporty — is gymnastics still worth it?

Especially then. Gymnastics builds concrete, visible confidence — roll, then handstand, then cartwheel — and teaches that ability grows through effort, not innate talent. The goal of recreational gymnastics isn't producing gymnasts; it's producing strong, coordinated, confident children, whatever they do next. See the recreational levels →
