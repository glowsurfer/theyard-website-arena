Why gymnastics · The evidence and The Yard's view

Why gymnastics comes first

Gymnastics is called the mother of all sports because it develops every fundamental movement pattern. It builds all-round strength, balance, coordination and spatial awareness, along with the resilience and focus that carry into school, other sports and the rest of a child's life, which is why we say The Yard teaches gymnastics and develops character.

Five kinds of benefit

What gymnastics
actually builds.

Physical Mental and emotional Cross-sport transfer Academic and cognitive Life skills
01 · Physical

A whole body,
built at once.

Few sports train the upper body, core and lower body in the same session. Gymnastics does it every week, from the very first class.

All-round functional strength

Upper body, core and lower body develop together, alongside flexibility, balance, coordination and spatial awareness, from a young age.

Stronger bones for life

As a high-impact, weight-bearing activity, gymnastics builds 10 to 40 percent higher bone density at key sites like the spine and hip, and the benefit persists into adulthood (Ward et al., Osteoporosis International).

Safe falling, a skill for life

Rolling and absorbing impact is one of the most transferable skills a child can learn, and it reduces injury risk in every sport they will ever play.

Start early: the WHO recommends gymnastics-type activities for developing fundamental movement skills in children aged 3 to 5. At The Yard, Gym Tots starts at 18 months.
Brave
02 · Mental and emotional

Real courage,
practised weekly.

Gymnastics is one of very few sports that asks children to overcome genuine fear, going upside down and leaving the ground, inside a safe and coached environment.

Resilience that is real

Inverting and leaving the ground are genuinely scary at first. Conquering that fear safely, week after week, builds a resilience few other activities can match, and what the coach does in the thirty seconds after a fall is the curriculum.

Visible confidence

A roll becomes a handstand, a handstand becomes a cartwheel, and one day a cartwheel becomes a backflip. Skill progression is concrete and visible, so confidence is earned.

A growth mindset

Gymnastics teaches that ability is built through effort, one small step compounded on the last, which is the mindset that pays off everywhere else in a child's life.

Focus and composure

Sustained focus, discipline and multi-step instructions, plus healthy exposure to structured competition, scoring and handling setbacks.

03 · Cross-sport transfer

The mother of
all sports.

The International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) calls gymnastics the mother of all sports because it develops every fundamental movement pattern: locomotion, stability and manipulation.

Children who do gymnastics as a base before specialising in another sport have better long-term athletic outcomes and fewer injuries (Lloyd et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine).

That is why the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends early diversification through activities like gymnastics ahead of specialising in one sport before age 12. Gymnastics is the foundation under your child's future sport, whatever that sport turns out to be.

04 · Academic and cognitive

Stronger in the gym,
sharper in class.

The link between movement and learning is well studied, and gymnastics sits at the strong end of it.

Executive function

Cognitively demanding physical activity like gymnastics boosts planning, focus and working memory more than simple exercise like running (Pesce et al., Health Psychology).

Working memory

Learning routines and skill sequences exercises working memory, the same capacity behind following instructions in the classroom.

Classroom carry-over

Physically active children show improved concentration, classroom behaviour and academic performance, and a CDC review found a positive link in over half of the studies it examined.

05 · Life skills

Lessons that outlast
the leotard.

Whether or not a child stays in gymnastics, what they learn on the floor goes with them. That is why we coach the way we do, foundations before sophistication, and why a child who leaves us strong, coordinated and confident has had a successful time at The Yard, whatever sport comes next.

Perseverance

Gymnastics has a high attempt-to-mastery ratio, so children learn, viscerally, that repeated effort leads to results.

Independence

In the end a gymnast performs alone. Owning your own effort starts early and sticks, and our coaches model it by owning theirs in front of the children.

Social skills

Group training means taking turns, encouraging peers and respecting coaches in every single session.

Time management

Balancing training with school commitments builds habits that serve teenagers well beyond sport.

Parents ask

Why-gymnastics questions

Why is gymnastics called the mother of all sports?

The International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) calls gymnastics the mother of all sports because it develops every fundamental movement pattern: locomotion, stability and manipulation. Children who build this physical-literacy base before specialising in another sport show better long-term athletic outcomes and fewer injuries (Lloyd et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine).

What age should a child start gymnastics?

Early. The WHO recommends gymnastics-type activities for developing fundamental movement skills in children aged 3 to 5, and the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends early diversification through activities like gymnastics ahead of specialising in a single sport before age 12. At The Yard, Gym Tots starts at 18 months and no experience is needed at any entry level.

Will gymnastics help my child in other sports?

Yes. Gymnastics skills transfer directly: aerial awareness to diving, balance and explosive power to martial arts, core strength and body tension to swimming, agility and safe falling to football and rugby, flexibility and body line to dance, grip strength and proprioception to climbing, and explosive power and jumping to athletics.

Does gymnastics help with school and concentration?

Cognitively demanding physical activity like gymnastics boosts executive function (planning, focus and working memory) more than simple exercise like running (Pesce et al., Health Psychology). Learning routines exercises working memory, and a CDC review found a positive link between physical activity and academic performance in over half of studies.

Is gymnastics safe for young children?

In a properly coached environment, gymnastics is one of the best injury-prevention investments a child can make: it teaches safe falling, rolling and absorbing impact, which reduces injury risk in every sport. At The Yard, every class is run by a qualified coach on FIG-certified apparatus at roughly one coach to eight children (one to six for KinderTots).

My child is shy about sport. Is gymnastics still worth it?

Especially then. Gymnastics builds concrete, visible confidence, from a roll to a handstand to a cartwheel, and teaches that ability grows through effort. At The Yard progress is the promise and the pace belongs to the child, and the goal of recreational gymnastics is a strong, coordinated, confident child, whatever they do next.
