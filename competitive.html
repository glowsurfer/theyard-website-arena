The road to the podium

The Yard's competitive Women's and Men's Artistic Gymnastics pathway is selection-based: entry is by a free assessment and an invitation. Squads train 6 to 24 hours a week on SGLP and USAG standards (Levels 1 to 10) over an August to July season, and they train at all four Yard centres, with the club's meets hosted at Jurong, our High-Performance Arena. Play to podium is one pathway, and this is its summit.

Two disciplines

Women's and Men's
Artistic Gymnastics.

Both disciplines follow the SGLP (Singapore Gymnastics Levels Programme) and USAG standards, progressing from compulsory routines to optional ones. Squad training means more hours, smaller coach-to-athlete ratios, advanced skill and routine work, and mandatory competitions. Every athlete hears one plan from every coach, because our coaches settle their differences before they reach the floor.

Four apparatusBy selection

Women's Artistic (WAG)

Vault, uneven bars, balance beam and floor exercise. WAG training builds strength, agility and grace, and beside the physical work it builds the composure that competition demands, one rep, one plateau and one breakthrough at a time.

  • SGLP and USAG Levels 1 to 10, from compulsory to optional routines
  • Women's Artistic squads at all four centres, Men's Artistic at Dover and Jurong

Entry and selection

Entry begins with
a free assessment.

Entry to a squad starts with a free assessment arranged directly with the centre team, where coaches evaluate current ability and potential, and suitable children are invited into a squad. Members join a club here, and the club turns up for them.

  • Selection is coach-led. The typical route is a child progressing well in recreational classes, a coach noticing, then an assessment with the squad group.
  • Athletes join at the SGLP and USAG squad level that matches their ability, which may be well above the entry squad.
  • Relocating families are welcome. Australian (ALP) and US (USAG) grades generally translate, and final placement is always by The Yard's own assessment.
  • Placement is earned continuously. Athletes are evaluated on attendance, effort, progress, coachability and behaviour, and we teach them to own their outcomes the way our coaches own theirs.
  • Stepping back is a legitimate choice. An athlete who wants to scale back can move to a recreational level that fits, and the door stays open.

Level 10
The level framework

How SGLP and USAG
levels work.

Singapore's national framework runs from Foundation to Level 10, from set compulsory routines to fully optional routines the athlete and coach design together. Above Level 10 sits international representation under FIG and Junior FIG rules.

Foundation Programme First step

Where squad life begins: building the strength, flexibility and base shapes that everything above depends on. Foundations before sophistication, which is the same philosophy as the rest of The Yard.

Levels 1 and 2 Early skills

Early competitive skills on each apparatus, trained to consistent, correct technique, with no formal compulsory routines yet.

Levels 3 to 5 Compulsory routines

Set compulsory routines with set skills, choreography and music. Every athlete performs the same routine and is judged on execution, so these are the precision and repetition years.

Levels 6 to 8 Mixed compulsory and optional

A mix of compulsory and optional elements with no or limited bonus. Athletes start shaping routines around their own strengths.

Level 9 Optional, limited bonus

Optional routines with limited bonus scoring: high-level skills in individually composed routines.

Level 10 The national peak

Full optional routines with unlimited bonus and set composition requirements. This is the highest national level before international representation.

Beyond: FIG and Junior FIG International

Above the national framework sits international competition under the FIG Code of Points. On the MAG side, the national programme uses Stages and Levels, and its 2025 revamp introduced Stage 6 with Modified Junior FIG elements.

Placement is decided at assessment. Arrange one with the team.

Good to know

Competitive questions

How does my child join a competitive squad?

Entry is by assessment and invitation. The assessment is free of charge and is arranged directly with the centre team. Coaches evaluate current skills, conditioning and potential, and suitable children are invited into a WAG or MAG squad.

Is the competitive assessment really free?

Yes. Recreational classes start with a paid trial, while entry to the competitive pathway starts with a free assessment arranged with the centre team.

Does every athlete start at the lowest squad level?

No. Athletes are placed at the SGLP and USAG squad level that matches their current ability. Families relocating from Australia (ALP) or the United States (USAG) will find grades generally translate, and final placement is always by The Yard's own assessment.

How many hours per week do squads train?

Squads train 6 to 24 hours per week depending on level. Squad training means smaller coach-to-athlete ratios, advanced skill and routine work, and mandatory competitions.

When is the competitive season?

The season runs August to July, aligned with Singapore's national competition calendar. The Yard hosts the Diamond Classic (invitational, March) and the sanctioned Lion City Classic (April) at its Jurong arena. See the events calendar.

Can my child join the pathway from any Yard centre?

Yes. The pathway is open to children progressing through recreational classes at any of the four centres. Selection is coach-led: coaches put children forward for assessment, and parents can also ask their coach for a conversation. Squads train at all four centres, so your child can usually stay at the centre they know. Women's Artistic squads run at every centre, and Men's Artistic squads run at Dover and Jurong.

Does an invitation guarantee a permanent squad place?

No. Athletes are continuously evaluated on attendance, effort, progress, coachability and behaviour. Continued placement is earned, which is part of what makes the pathway work.
