The Competitive Pathway

The Yard's competitive Women's and Men's Artistic Gymnastics pathway is selection-based: entry is by a free assessment and invitation, not open enrolment. Squads train 6–24 hours per week on SGLP and USAG standards (Levels 1–10) over an August–July season, with the High Performance hub at The Yard Jurong.

Two disciplines

WAG & MAG.

Both disciplines follow the SGLP (Singapore Gymnastics Levels Programme) and USAG standards, progressing from compulsory to optional routines. Squad training means more hours, smaller coach-to-athlete ratios, advanced skill and routine work, and mandatory competitions.

Entry & selection

An assessment,
not a trial.

Competitive squads don't take trial bookings. Entry starts with a free assessment arranged directly with the centre team — coaches evaluate current ability and potential, and suitable children are invited into a squad.

  • Coach-led, not enquiry-led — the typical route is a child progressing well in recreational classes, a coach noticing, then an assessment with the squad group.
  • Placed at the right level — athletes join at the appropriate SGLP/USAG squad level for their ability, not necessarily the lowest.
  • Relocating families welcome — Australian (ALP) and US (USAG) grades generally translate, but final placement is always by The Yard's own assessment.
  • Placement is earned continuously — athletes are evaluated on attendance, effort, progress, coachability and behaviour.

The level framework

How SGLP & USAG
levels work

Singapore's national framework runs from Foundation to Level 10 — from set compulsory routines to fully optional routines the athlete and coach design. Above Level 10 sits international (FIG / Junior FIG) representation.

Foundation Programme First step

Where squad life begins: building the strength, flexibility and base shapes that everything above depends on. Foundations before sophistication — the same philosophy as the rest of The Yard.

Levels 1–2 Early skills

Early competitive skills on each apparatus, trained to consistent, correct technique.

Levels 3–5 Compulsory routines

Set compulsory routines — every athlete performs the same routine, judged on execution. Precision and repetition years.

Levels 6–8 Mixed compulsory / optional

A mix of compulsory and optional work — athletes start shaping routines around their own strengths.

Level 9 Optional, limited bonus

Optional routines with limited bonus scoring — high-level skills, individually composed routines.

Level 10 The national peak

Full optional routines with unlimited bonus — the highest national level before international representation.

Beyond: FIG / Junior FIG International

Above the national framework sits international competition under FIG rules. On the MAG side, the national programme uses Stages/Levels — the 2025 revamp introduced Stage 6 with Modified Junior FIG elements.

Where does your child fit? Placement is decided at assessment — arrange one.

Good to know

Competitive FAQs

How does my child join a competitive squad?

Entry is by assessment and invitation, not open enrolment. The assessment is free of charge — it is not a trial class — and is arranged directly with the centre team. Coaches evaluate current skills, conditioning and potential, and suitable children are invited into a WAG or MAG squad.

Is the competitive assessment really free?

Yes. Unlike recreational classes, which start with a paid trial, entry to the competitive pathway starts with a free assessment arranged with the centre team.

Does every athlete start at the lowest squad level?

No. Athletes are placed at the appropriate SGLP/USAG squad level for their current ability — not necessarily the lowest. Families relocating from Australia (ALP) or the United States (USAG) will find grades generally translate, but final placement is always by The Yard's own assessment.

How many hours per week do squads train?

Squads train 6 to 24 hours per week depending on level. Squad training means smaller coach-to-athlete ratios, advanced skill and routine work, and mandatory competitions.

When is the competitive season?

The season runs August to July, aligned with Singapore's national competition calendar. The Yard hosts the Diamond Classic (invitational, March) and the sanctioned Lion City Classic (April) at its Jurong arena.

Can my child join the pathway from any Yard centre?

Yes — the pathway is open to children progressing through recreational classes at any of the four centres. Selection is coach-led: coaches put children forward for assessment, and parents can also ask their coach for a conversation. The High Performance hub is at The Yard Jurong, with WAG Kinder Development also running at Dempsey.

Does an invitation guarantee a permanent squad place?

No. Athletes are continuously evaluated on attendance, effort, progress, coachability and behaviour. Continued placement is earned, which is part of what makes the pathway work.
